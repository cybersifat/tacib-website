PRACTICAL CYBER SAFETY
GUIDE · EDUCATION

What to do when an account is compromised.

Move carefully. Your email account often controls password resets for many other services, so prioritize the recovery channel and avoid sharing verification codes with anyone.

If you suspect the device itself is monitored or unsafe, use a trusted device and seek help from someone you trust before changing settings that could increase your risk.

1. Secure the recovery email first

From a device you trust, change the password for the primary email account if you still control it. Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers. Remove unfamiliar options and check for forwarding rules or app access you did not create.

2. Use the service's official recovery process

Type the official service address yourself or use its verified app. Follow its account recovery instructions. Do not pay an unsolicited “recovery expert” or share one-time codes, backup codes, passwords, or session cookies.

3. Revoke unknown access

  • Sign out unknown sessions and devices.
  • Review connected apps and remove access you do not recognize.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication using a method you can safely maintain.
  • Review account email, phone number, username, and privacy settings.

4. Reduce follow-on harm

Warn close contacts that suspicious messages from the account may not be yours. If the account is used for payments or business, notify relevant providers through their official channels and review recent activity.

5. Preserve key details and report

Record the account name, profile URL, approximate timeline, suspicious login notices, and report reference numbers. Use the platform's official reporting channel. If the incident may fall within a Bangladesh official reporting body's scope, review the options on our Cyber Help & Reporting page ↗.

Do not

  • Share passwords, recovery codes, or identity documents with unknown people.
  • Install remote-access software for a person who contacted you unexpectedly.
  • Run retaliation or “hack-back” attempts.

This guide is general education and cannot restore an account or substitute for the platform's official recovery process.
